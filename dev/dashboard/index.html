Dashboard¶

Arranging visuals is a WPF concern: the report (easybi/v1) never contains a layout. The dashboard is built in three layers, each usable on its own.

The layout (DashboardLayoutDefinition) is a stack of bands, each with a mode. Its items are greedy: without a constraint, they share the room their neighbours leave, so a dashboard never has a gap.

  • Fit bands are a grid of 12 columns that fills the width. A band is a stack of lines; a line is an item, or a container (LayoutNode.Row: side by side, LayoutNode.Column: stacked) of items and containers. Along a container, constrained children keep their size (ColumnSpan in columns, RowSpan in rows) and the others share the rest; across it, every child takes the whole room. An item with a RowSpan sets the height of its line and the items next to it stretch, so items stacked beside a tall item share its height.
  • Column tiers: on a narrow panel, Fit bands show fewer columns (DashboardPanel.ColumnTiers; by default 1 column under 600 px and 6 under 960 px, ColumnTier.Defaults). Spans are scaled to the tier, a line whose items would get less than 3 columns each is broken into several lines (one item per line in a single column), and constrained items keep their proportions while filling their line. Only the display adapts: the saved layout does not change, and a resize in a tier is saved in the layout's columns (DashboardPanel.ActiveColumns, ToLayoutSpan).
  • Wrap bands start their items at DashboardPanel.MinItemWidth (or their fixed Width in pixels), break lines when the next one does not fit, then share what is left on each line. Scroll bands do the same on one line, and scroll horizontally when the items do not fit.
  • A container whose children are all constrained leaves a free space, which the dashboard offers to fill.
new DashboardLayoutDefinition
{
    Bands =
    [
        new BandLayout
        {
            Items =
            [
                new LayoutNode { VisualId = title.Id, RowSpan = 1 },                                // a whole line
                LayoutNode.Row(
                    new LayoutNode { VisualId = map.Id, ColumnSpan = 6, RowSpan = 6 },               // left half, 2 lines high
                    LayoutNode.Column(LayoutNode.Item(sales.Id), LayoutNode.Item(margin.Id))),       // stacked beside it
            ],
        },
        new BandLayout { Mode = BandMode.Scroll, Items = [LayoutNode.Item(a.Id), new LayoutNode { VisualId = b.Id, Width = 360 }] },
    ],
};

DashboardPanel shows a layout (Layout property); children are matched to its items by the attached DashboardPanel.ItemId. The geometry itself is a pure function (BandArrangement.Arrange), and the panel exposes hit tests for interactions (EdgeAt, FlowIndexAt, FreeSpaces, and CaptureGeometry to hit-test the layout from before a drag).

DashboardLayout is the static class that changes a panel's layout while keeping it consistent: AddNextTo, MoveNextTo (ItemSide.Right, Left, Below, Above), InsertAt / MoveTo (a new line), Resize, ResizeWidth, SetAuto, Remove, InsertBand, RemoveBand, SetBandMode, plus Update for custom operations, Capture (snapshot to restore) and GetLayout / SetLayout (to save and restore). Next to an item, a side along its container joins it (the line's columns are shared when it has no room left), a side across it wraps the item and the newcomer in a new container. The rules are pure functions on records in DashboardLayoutEngine, usable without any UI.

DashboardView is the ready-made (lookless) control: DashboardItems showing visuals in a DashboardPanel, with every interaction provided by a behavior (Microsoft.Xaml.Behaviors):

Behavior Feature
AddButtonsBehavior "+" on the right and bottom edges of an item, "+" between lines and between bands
FreeSpaceBehavior fills free spaces with a placeholder that adds an item there
FieldDropBehavior makes visuals from fields dragged from a FieldList (see below)
MoveBehavior grip to drag an item: a picture follows the pointer and a placeholder shows where it lands, the others making room live; near an item's edge it goes next to it (like "+"), between two lines it becomes a line, over a free space it fills it; across bands, auto-scroll near the edges; Escape cancels
ResizeBehavior corner handle: constrains the item (columns and rows in a grid band, pixels and rows elsewhere)
ItemToolbarBehavior automatic size (for a resized item), edit, remove; text size, bold, alignment; image fit
SelectionBehavior click selects an item (Ctrl+click adds or removes it, except on a bar, slice or row of a chart, where it extends the cross-filtering: VisualView.DataSelectedEvent), click on the background unselects, drag from the background draws a selection box; arrows go to the nearest item (Shift adds it), Escape unselects
KeyboardLayoutBehavior Ctrl+arrows move the selected item, Ctrl+Shift+Down/Up put it in a new band, Alt+arrows extend the selected items over the free room next to them (through the commands below)
BandMenuBehavior ⋮ menu of a band: Fit / Scroll / Wrap, insert, remove (MenuOpening to customize it)
BandScrollBehavior scroll bar and Shift+wheel for scrolling bands
TextEditBehavior double-click a text to edit it in place
UndoRedoBehavior Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y (ApplicationCommands.Undo / Redo) 
<bi:DashboardView x:Name="Dashboard" Context="{Binding Context}" Document="{Binding Dashboard}">
    <i:Interaction.Behaviors>
        <!-- Declaring a behavior replaces its default; set UseDefaultBehaviors="False" to pick them all -->
        <bi:UndoRedoBehavior MaxHistory="100" />
    </i:Interaction.Behaviors>
</bi:DashboardView>

Hooks for applications:

  • events EditVisualRequested (replace the built-in designer dialog), ImageRequested (replace the file dialog), TextEditRequested, ItemRemoving (veto), Changed (every change, with the documents before and after), DocumentLoaded;
  • the item selection: SelectedItems, SelectionChanged, Select(items, DashboardSelectionMode.Replace/Add/Toggle), SelectAll, UnselectAll, RemoveSelected and DuplicateSelected (one change each), also reached through ApplicationCommands.Delete (Del), ApplicationCommands.SelectAll (Ctrl+A) and DashboardCommands.Duplicate (Ctrl+D); DashboardItem.IsSelected for templates (the default one draws a ring) and the DashboardOverlay.SelectionBoxStyleKey style. A read-only dashboard has no selection;
  • lazy loading: only the visuals of the items in or near the visible part of the dashboard query their data (DashboardItem.IsInViewport, bound to VisualView.IsActive by the default template); the others query once they scroll into view, a change of filters or data giving them one query then. ViewportMargin sets how far ahead items load (one viewport by default), LoadsVisibleItemsOnly = false loads them all (to print or export, for instance). VisualView.IsActive = false holds back the queries of any visual;
  • keyboard layout: MoveSelected(side, toNewBand) (DashboardCommands.MoveItem and MoveItemToNewBand, Ctrl+arrows and Ctrl+Shift+Down/Up; DashboardLayoutEngine.Step): Down takes an item sharing its line to a new line below; alone on its line, it joins the next line, and from the last line it goes into the band below (created past the last one); Up the same upwards, so that Down and Up undo each other; Ctrl+Shift+Down/Up puts it in a new band below/above; Left/Right swap it with its neighbour. ExtendSelected(width) (DashboardCommands.ExtendItem, Alt+arrows; DashboardLayoutEngine.Fill) extends the selected items over the free room next to them without shrinking their neighbours;
  • copy and paste of the selected items, possibly between dashboards: CopySelected / CutSelected / Paste (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, Ctrl+V through ApplicationCommands; CreateCopy and Paste(data) work without the clipboard). The clipboard holds an easybi/v1 report (format VisualTransfer.ClipboardFormat) with the visuals and the calculated measures they use, plus their titles as text, and their layout (format DashboardView.LayoutClipboardFormat: their arrangement and sizes, DashboardLayoutEngine.Extract). Pasted items get new ids, keep their arrangement and sizes, and go after the line of the last selected item, or at the end (DashboardLayoutEngine.Insert; lines of a grid band join the anchor's grid band, other bands come as new bands); without the layout format, each one goes to the right of the previous one. Visuals of a model the context does not know are left out, and a calculated measure whose name is taken by another definition is renamed (VisualTransfer in EasyBI.Core);
  • DashboardCommands (AddVisual, AddText, AddImage, EditItem, RemoveItem, ResetSize, AddRight, AddBelow, InsertBand, RemoveBand, Duplicate, MoveItem, MoveItemToNewBand, ExtendItem — the last three take an ItemSide or its name, as CommandParameter="Below"...) for custom templates, and public methods (AddNextTo, AddIn a free space, InsertLine, SetAutoSize, SetVisual, InsertBand, BeginEdit to group changes into one undo step...);
  • a default template for DashboardView (its Panel in a scroll viewer, under an AdornerDecorator where behaviors draw: a custom template shows it with <ContentPresenter Content="{Binding Panel, RelativeSource={RelativeSource TemplatedParent}}" />), an implicit style for DashboardItem (lookless control) and the DashboardOverlay style keys (PlusButtonStyleKey, IconButtonStyleKey, GripStyleKey, ResizeGripStyleKey, FreeSpaceStyleKey, DropPlaceholderStyleKey, DragGhostStyleKey) to restyle;
  • DashboardBehavior as a base class for new interactions: it gives access to the view, the panel and the overlay where behaviors draw their controls; DragGesture handles press-drag-release gestures (with auto-scroll: AutoScroll, ScrollsBands).

IsReadOnly="True" hides the editing chrome; clicks keep cross-filtering in every mode.

Building visuals by dragging fields. FieldList shows the fields of the context's models (model picker, dimensions, measures) and drags them as ModelFields. Placed next to a dashboard, it lets users build it directly:

<DockPanel>
    <bi:FieldList DockPanel.Dock="Left" Width="210" Context="{Binding Context}" />
    <bi:DashboardView Context="{Binding Context}" />
</DockPanel>
  • dropped where an item can go (an edge, between two lines, a free space, a scrolling or wrapping band), a field makes a new visual: a KPI for a measure, the first measure by that dimension for a dimension (a line by month for a date, bars otherwise); the other items make room live around a placeholder;
  • dropped on an empty item, it fills it; dropped on a visual, it is added to it (a measure to its values, a dimension to its axis, then its legend; a dimension breaks a KPI down into bars);
  • the rules are pure functions in FieldVisuals (Create, AddField); FieldDropBehavior.CreateVisual and AddField replace them. Each drop is one undo step.

The designer's field panel is the same FieldList (its shelves accept the ModelFields of the selected model). A search box at the top of the field list (FieldList.Search) and above the values of a filter (from 8 values on, FilterItemViewModel.Search; checked values that are not shown stay checked) narrow the lists down: case and accents are ignored and every word must appear (TextMatch.Matches in EasyBI.Core).

In the designer, the visual is sorted from the properties of a chip of the Axis or Values well (ascending ↑ or descending ↓; one sorted field at a time, the sort following the field when its aggregation or grain changes). The visual's properties recall the active sort and limit the visual to its first rows (Top). DashboardDropTarget.Find, the place an item dropped at a point goes, is shared by moves and field drops.
